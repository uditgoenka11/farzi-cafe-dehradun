A Local's Food Guide to Dehradun

By The Farzi Café Dehradun Team · 22 May 2026

Dehradun is small enough to walk through in a weekend and old enough to keep secrets. We've been feeding people on its Rajpur Road rooftop for a few seasons now, and our team — many of them Doon-born — eats their way through the city on every off-day. Below is the unofficial Farzi Café guide. Skip the chains, follow the locals.

Breakfast: bun-tikki at the Paltan Bazaar carts

Forget your omelette. The Doon breakfast is bun-tikki — a soft, slightly sweet bread bun split, slathered with butter and chutney, and stuffed with a hot aloo tikki. Best carts are at the western end of Paltan Bazaar, opposite the old clock tower. Get there before 9 AM or you'll be queueing behind LBSNAA cadets.

Late morning: momos at Char Dukan, Landour

Yes, technically Mussoorie. But if you're driving up Rajpur Road on a clear morning, push another forty minutes and you'll find Char Dukan — four shops at the top of Landour serving the country's most addictive cheese-momos. Order steamed, not fried, and have one round of the chilli-garlic dip before you over-commit to plain.

Lunch: thali at Kumar's, Rajpur

Two kilometres from our rooftop, on the older stretch of Rajpur Road, sits Kumar's — an unmarked Garhwali thali joint that does mandua roti, gahat dal, kafuli (a spinach-and-paneer curry) and bhang ki chutney. Their lunch service starts at 12:30, ends at 3, and they refuse to do takeaway. Sit, eat, walk it off with a slow climb up Rajpur.

Afternoon coffee: the indie cafés on EC Road

Doon has a quietly excellent third-wave coffee scene. Our pick is Brewer's Café on EC Road — a single-origin Coorg pour-over with a slice of orange polenta cake will reset your afternoon. They take cash only, and their playlist is unforgivably good.

Sundowner: Farzi Café Dehradun (we couldn't resist)

The sun sets behind the Mussoorie hills around 6:45 PM in summer. Pull up a stool at the onyx bar, order a Saffron Sour, and watch the cabanas light up one by one. We have a window for sundowner pricing on cocktails Monday to Thursday between 5 and 7 PM — house spirits at ₹ 295.

Dinner: depends on your mood

If you want modern Indian, stay where you are. If you want regional, head to Sushi-no-Doon for the surprisingly good izakaya menu, or to Brick Oven in Astley Hall for a Neapolitan pizza. If you want street food, Punjabi tadka at Daddy's Dhaba on Rajpur Road is loud, cheap and exactly right.

Late-night: Doon-style chow mein at the Sahranpur Chowk vendors

After 10 PM, the food carts at Sahranpur Chowk light up and serve a Doon-specific chow mein — thinner noodles, more vinegar, an extra slug of soy. It costs ₹ 80, comes in a paper bowl, and is the best closing chapter to a long evening. Wear sneakers. Bring tissues.

For the sweet tooth

Two stops:

  • Ellora's on Rajpur Road — for fresh patties (the chicken puff is iconic) and a black-forest pastry that hasn't changed since 1985.
  • Kumar Sweet Shop in Paltan Bazaar — for bal mithai (the Kumaoni walnut-fudge sweet) and a slow-melt singori wrapped in maalu leaf.

One thing not to do

Do not try to do this entire list in one weekend. Dehradun is best eaten the way it's lived — slowly, with naps between meals, and a long walk down Rajpur Road at golden hour.

And when you're ready for a meal that ties all of these flavours together — our team will be on the rooftop, with the kettle on.
